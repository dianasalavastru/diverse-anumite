# VISUAL_DIRECTION_v2.0

**The creative-direction authority for the visual layer — anchored to the frozen brand identity, governed by the metaphor of *measured reality*.**
Status: **authoritative** · governs all future visual implementation · 2026-07-30

This document defines **how** the frozen architecture (Documentation v1.0) is expressed visually, using the **existing identity created by the designer** — electric blue, pixel typography, the mascot, white ground, floating compositions, a technical/digital aesthetic. That identity is **frozen and non-negotiable**. This is an **evolution, not a redesign**: someone who knows the current design must instantly recognize it as the same brand — only more mature, more confident, more timeless.

**Relationship to Documentation v1.0.** v1.0 is supreme on *what* and *where*. This document governs only *how it looks, feels, and behaves*. It changes none of v1.0.

**The reference, correctly framed.** Septiembre Arquitectura is an influence on **direction**, never on appearance. We take its *discipline* — hierarchy, curation, restraint, pacing — and we reject its *look*. Whenever a principle here could be traced to "the reference does this," it has been re-justified from our own identity or removed.

## Revision record

This version supersedes an earlier draft after a formal creative-director review. The changes, and why:

- **Guiding metaphor re-rooted from "architectural publication" to "measured reality."** The print metaphor (monograph, catalogue, colophon, marginalia, the "gallery wall") was a borrowing from the reference's world. We are an architecture + digital-measurement studio; our metaphor must be ours.
- **A warmth caveat added to the metaphor.** Measured reality must never feel cold — precision is always balanced by warmth, personality, and human craftsmanship; the technology documents architecture, it never replaces it. This guards against drifting from "architecture studio using amazing technology" into "cool technology company."
- **Editorial split into *discipline* vs *aesthetic*.** We keep editorial as a discipline (curation, hierarchy, restraint, authored composition); we reject editorial as a printed-object look.
- **"Less blue" replaced by "blue as semantic activation."** Blue now has a *meaning* and a rule, not a quota.
- **Focus elevated to the universal hierarchy mechanism.** Hierarchy is a state (often interactive), not only a static composition.
- **Motion reframed as a *primary material*, used deliberately — not a mandatory principle.** Removed the anti-motion stance.
- **Discovery added as a core value alongside calm.** The experience must reward curiosity and inspection, not just observation.
- **The dual white/dark environment is recorded as a prototype candidate, not a principle** — promising, to be validated in practice before elevation.
- **Print vocabulary stripped throughout.** Metadata is reframed as measurement data.

## 0 · Foundations

### 0.1 Guiding metaphor — Measured Reality

The site should feel like **a precise instrument for exploring and composing space** — made by people who *measure reality* (scanning, photogrammetry, drone capture, planșe, rendering) and design within it. Not a printed book; a calm, exact, screen-native environment that invites inspection.

**Measured reality should never feel cold.** Precision is always balanced by warmth, personality and human craftsmanship. The technology documents architecture—it never replaces it. The instrument exists to reveal the things that make architecture emotional — the light, the textures, the spaces, the people, the stories, the imperfections — never to become the hero itself.

*Why this metaphor:* it is unmistakably ours, owned by no competitor and no template. It re-roots every downstream decision — colour, type, motion, imagery, interaction — in the studio's actual craft rather than in a reference aesthetic. And it resolves the central tension of the brand: architecture (composition, judgement, warmth) *and* technology (precision, data, measurement) held in one confident voice.

### 0.2 Two core values in balance — Calm and Discovery

The experience is governed by two values, weighted equally:

**Calm** — precision, restraint, confidence, quiet. The interface behaves like a well-damped instrument: nothing jitters, nothing shouts, nothing is rushed. *Why:* calm lowers the visitor's defences, signals a practice confident enough not to oversell, and lets the work register.

**Discovery** — curiosity, inspection, exploration. The visitor is an **explorer and inspector**, not a passive observer. Work can be zoomed, rotated, focused, revealed; the site rewards the impulse to look closer. *Why:* the studio's entire proposition is *exploring architecture through digital measurement* — the experience must embody that, not merely describe it. Discovery is what turns viewing into evaluating, and evaluating is what earns trust.

Held together: **calm without discovery is inert; discovery without calm is noise.** The site is calm *so that* discovery feels effortless and inviting.

**How it should feel over time.** *Moment 0* — calm precision and curiosity: an instrument that invites rather than explains. *Minute 1* — effortless clarity: one serious practice that measures and composes reality. *Minute 3* — active exploration: focusing, zooming, inspecting real work, a rhythm of looking closer. *Minute 10* — conviction reached through the visitor's own discovery, so contact arrives as a natural next step, not a prompt. The arc: **calm → clarity → exploration → conviction.**

### 0.3 Editorial as discipline, not aesthetic

We adopt editorial **discipline**: curation over listing, hierarchy over uniformity, restraint over decoration, authored composition over filled containers, deliberate pacing over even spacing. We reject editorial **aesthetic**: the printed-object look, the monochrome-publication surface, the vocabulary of catalogues and colophons. *Why:* the discipline is what produces confidence and timelessness; the aesthetic would make us look like the reference and unlike ourselves. This single distinction guards every future decision.

## 1 · Brand Preservation Charter — KEEP 100%

Fixed. Each note gives the element's **role** and the **discipline** that makes it feel premium.

**Electric blue** — the brand's signal. Its role is defined precisely in §2.1: it marks *activation and measurement*, never area or decoration. Discipline: **semantic, not decorative.** Used with meaning, it can be fully confident in its rare moments.

**Pixel typography** — the brand's display and identity voice; also our *coordinate/label* voice (§2.5). Role: wordmark, section markers, numerals, wayfinding, the mascot's world. Discipline: **a voice, never the body.**

**The mascot** (giraffe, drone, and other contextual variants) — the brand's character and warmth, and a potential adaptive wayfinding device. It is the clearest carrier of the human, playful side that keeps the technology warm. Discipline: **a character, not a logo dropped in corners.**

**White ground** — the working surface on which measured work is composed and inspected. Discipline: **protect it;** heavy filled fields are deliberate events, never default containers. (A darker "capture" environment is a prototype candidate — §12, not yet a rule.)

**Floating compositions** — the brand's native layout instinct. Discipline: **float with hierarchy** (one element leads), edge-aware, allowed to bleed off the canvas.

**Technical / digital aesthetic** — point clouds, scans, planșe, drone imagery. Role: authentic proof of the measured-reality craft. Discipline: **treated as work to inspect, with precision and calm** — never as a gadget demo, and always in service of revealing the architecture, not displacing it.

**Brand personality** — technical, exact, quietly playful, confident, contemporary, human. Everything below serves it.

## 2 · The operating system of the visual language

Five mechanisms that are *ours*. They are the engine; the recommendation sections that follow are their application.

### 2.1 Blue as semantic activation

**Electric blue is the signal of activation and measurement** — conceptually, the studio's scan-line, data-point, and selection highlight. It is permitted where something is *activated, measured, selected, revealed, or actioned*: the identity mark, the single primary action, the focused element, the moment a value or detail is revealed, a live/interactive state. It is **forbidden as area, fill, background, or decoration**, and forbidden on stacks of buttons.

*Why:* giving blue a meaning solves "overused blue" through logic rather than suppression — each blue moment becomes an intentional signal the visitor learns to read, which reads as precision and confidence. It ties the brand's colour to the brand's actual craft (measurement), which no template can imitate. And because it is rare, it can be saturated and bold where it does appear.

### 2.2 Focus as the universal hierarchy mechanism

**Wherever more than one thing exists, one is in focus — "measured" — and the rest recede.** The focused element is larger, at full contrast, and may carry the blue signal; the unfocused are smaller and lower-contrast but remain visible for context. Hierarchy is therefore a **state**, frequently interactive (a carousel's centre, a hovered item, a selected project), not only a fixed composition.

*Why:* focus is a single, coherent grammar that expresses curation everywhere — galleries, documentation sets, project browse — and it is native to a digital, interactive brand in a way static print composition is not. It directly serves both values: it creates calm (one clear subject at a time) and discovery (the visitor moves focus to explore). It is the mechanism that replaces equal-weight grids across the entire site.

### 2.3 Motion as a primary material — deliberate, not mandatory

**Motion is one of the brand's primary materials, alongside space and time — used deliberately wherever it improves understanding or exploration, and omitted where it does not.** Its character is instrument-grade: slow, weighted, precise, eased; it clarifies structure, enables inspection (focus transitions, zoom, a rotating capture), and never entertains. Reduced-motion is fully honored, and no single motion is load-bearing — but motion is *embraced as core to a digital-measurement identity*, not rationed as a rare exception.

*Why:* our work is literally moving, measured, spatial data; motion is a truthful expression of the craft and a primary tool of discovery. Framing it as a material (used with judgement) rather than a mandate keeps it from becoming decoration while still giving it the central role a digital studio deserves.

### 2.4 Silence as space *and* time; the reveal as inspection

Calm is created by **space and time together** — generous, composed emptiness *and* slow, precise transitions and pacing. A held moment and an unhurried reveal are our equivalent of a wide margin.

The **reveal is an act of inspection.** Information surfaces as if *measured* — a value, a detail, a label appearing as attention passes over it, like a sensor reading. Revelation is preferred over pre-printed labelling.

*Why:* time-based calm is native to a screen and to a measurement instrument; it is more original and more true than treating whitespace as the only material. Framing reveals as inspection makes every interaction a small demonstration of what the studio does, and it serves discovery directly.

### 2.5 Typography as measured data; pixel as coordinate markers

Project metadata (name, date, prize, team, location, type, specifications) is presented as **precise measurement data** — exact, consistent, quietly technical, like an instrument readout. The **pixel voice** is used at structural nodes — section markers, numerals, labels — as if labelling a measurement, paired always with a calm, legible reading voice for running text.

*Why:* reframing metadata as data (not "captions") is brand-native and reinforces the measured-reality metaphor; the pixel-as-coordinate device keeps the identity present and disciplined while tying it to the craft.

## 3 · Critique of the current design

The current design is **brand-strong and editorially underdeveloped.** The identity — pixel wordmark, electric blue, mascot, technical imagery — is distinctive and ownable, which is the hard part and already won. What is missing is the layer of *discipline and discovery*: hierarchy, pacing, focus, and inspection. As built, it reads closer to a well-branded **template** than to a measured, curated environment.

Concretely: the composition **stacks blocks of similar weight** down a central column (hero, floating images, a navy description band, a point cloud, an image row, a blue CTA block), so nothing leads and the eye is never directed. The **blue is doing too many jobs** — wordmark, CTA blocks, social buttons, contact button, states — which spends the signal and pulls the page toward marketing. **Text over-explains** where imagery and inspection could carry meaning. **Heavy filled bands** (navy description, black "LOGO" bars) interrupt the white and read as web-sections. Imagery sits at **mostly equal weight**, so the work never acquires the focus-driven hierarchy that makes it feel curated and explorable. None of this is an identity problem — it is a direction problem, fixable without touching a single brand element.

## 4 · Strengths to preserve

A **distinctive, ownable identity** (pixel + blue + mascot) — the scarcest asset in the category. **White ground + floating imagery** — the perfect substrate for focus hierarchy and asymmetry. **Authentic technical material** (point clouds, scans, drone, planșe) — real, inspectable proof of the craft. **The right interaction instincts** — hover-to-reveal, horizontal galleries with a focused centre, a rotating point cloud, a sequenced project page; these are discovery behaviours to refine, not replace. **Playfulness in a serious frame** (mascot, pixel type) — the warmth that keeps the technology human. **Tonal range** in photography — raw material for hierarchy.

## 5 · Weaknesses

**Flat hierarchy** (no focus, equal weight). **Over-used blue** (signal spent as decoration). **Uniform pacing** (no alternation of density and silence in space or time). **Over-explanation** (text doing the work of imagery and inspection). **Heavy filled bands** as containers. **Button/block CTA vocabulary** (SaaS register). **Equal-weight galleries** (merchandise, not curation). **Centred, narrow stacking** (inert, templated). Each is addressed by the operating system in §2 and the recommendations below.

## 6 · Concrete improvements per major moment

*Each moment keeps its frozen architectural responsibility and every brand element; only the visual behaviour matures.*

**The opening.** Let the wordmark and mascot operate as spatial elements anchored to the composition's edges with generous silence; open with **one dominant image** and much white — a fragment that invites, not a full explanation. Blue only on the wordmark here. *Why:* an edge-anchored, spacious opening reads as confident and creates curiosity; the single blue keeps the signal powerful.

**The floating compositions.** Introduce **focus/scale hierarchy** — one image leads, others support, edge-aligned, some bleeding off-canvas — with a diagonal reading path. *Why:* turns decorative floating into authored composition that expresses judgement and invites the eye to travel.

**The identity/description moment.** Move onto **white**, with a **short, confident** statement (not a paragraph) beside the **mascot used large.** *Why:* white + brevity + character reads as confidence and foregrounds personality; the long paragraph and dark band read as a corporate block.

**The reality-capture / point-cloud moment.** Give it a whole field and real silence as a **slow, explorable exhibit** — the actual measured work as the signature centerpiece, sequenced wide → detail, inviting inspection. *Why:* the studio's most impressive, most ownable content becomes the memorable moment, embodying discovery — while always revealing a real building, not just a data effect.

**The documentation sets.** Break uniform grids into **focus-driven, irregular compositions**; keep the **hover-to-blue reveal** as a slow *inspection* gesture. *Why:* focus makes it an explorable archive, not a product grid; the blue reveal is precise, on-brand, and content-serving.

**The project galleries.** **Focus carousels** — centred project dominant and blue-marked, neighbours smaller and lower-contrast, entering focus with a slow ≈1.0→1.05 zoom; metadata quiet, as measurement data. *Why:* curation and hierarchy through focus and motion — exploration, not a product slider — in the brand's own digital voice.

**The project page.** Keep the sound sequence (hero → metadata → planșe → photography → suggested), paced with **silence in space and time**; metadata as a quiet measurement readout; **planșe as an inspectable sequence with zoom**; large photography given room; suggested work as a quiet focus strip. *Why:* sequence + inspection turns a project into something explored and evaluated, and treats the technical drawings as evidence.

**The contact / primary action.** A **quiet invitation** with the action as a single precise **blue activation** — not a filled button block — surrounded by the most generous silence. *Why:* an invitation matches "understanding before persuasion"; the one blue action is the page's clear signal.

**The closing.** A composed ending — the mascot returned large, the wordmark, **quiet text links** for social and contact (not blue buttons), the EU-funding note small. *Why:* a composed close preserves the character and removes the SaaS-footer feel.

## 7 · Interaction & discovery

Interaction exists to enable **inspection and exploration**, calmly. **Focus carousels** as the browse pattern; **zoom** on planșe and detailed work; a **slow, explorable point cloud**; **hover/scan reveals** that surface information as if measured; **quiet, content-subordinate navigation.** Motion is slow, weighted, precise, single-run, and honored-when-reduced. Predictability everywhere; only the work surprises. *Why:* every interaction should let the visitor look closer — discovery is a core value — while remaining calm and instrument-like, which builds trust and keeps focus on the work.

## 8 · Rhythm & pacing (space + time)

Adopt a deliberate **DENSE ↔ SILENT** cadence, expressed in **both space and time**: entry (a strong single visual, much silence) → information (a compact, denser passage of measured data) → release (generous white, a pause) → content (work shown with focus hierarchy) → silence (a held emptiness, the exhale) → next (a quiet lead onward). Intervals **vary**; pacing is never metronomic; slow transitions carry calm as much as margins do. *Why:* alternation produces felt movement and lets each dense moment be legible; even spacing reads as a template.

## 9 · Image hierarchy (focus-driven)

**One image is in focus; the rest recede** — by scale, contrast, placement, crop, sequence, and, where interactive, focus state. A large ratio between dominant and subordinate, never timid. **Cropping is authorship** (never fit-to-slot). **Sequence as inspection** (wide → detail, scale contrast between neighbours, rest between sequences). **Tonal contrast used intentionally** (e.g. black-and-white for context, colour for the focused work). **Never a uniform equal grid by default.** *Why:* focus-driven hierarchy expresses curation and enables discovery natively; it is the §2.2 mechanism applied to imagery.

## 10 · Typography

**Keep the pixel voice — as identity and coordinate markers, never body**, with breathing room. **Pair it with a calm reading voice** for text. **Hierarchy through restraint and space, not size escalation.** **Metadata as measurement data** — precise, consistent, technical. **Blue removed from most type** — reserved for the wordmark and activation. *Why:* discipline reads as confidence; the pixel-as-coordinate device ties the identity to the craft; the calm reading voice makes the pixel accents land.

## 11 · Gallery & project presentation

**Curated, not listed** — an explorable collection with focus hierarchy, never a uniform portfolio grid. **Focus carousels** for browse (dominant centre, receding neighbours, slow zoom, quiet measurement-data metadata). **Irregular composition within a set** (one image leads). **The project page as an explorable sequence** (hero → metadata → inspectable planșe with zoom → large photography → quiet suggested strip), paced with silence. **Distinct character per work-nature** (the reality-capture collection and the design/competition collection may look different within the one system, while staying balanced in overall weight per the frozen co-equal pillars). **Reveal information on inspection** rather than pre-printing it. *Why:* focus + inspection create curation and discovery in the brand's digital voice; uniform grids erase both.

## 12 · Design principles, grammar, and guardrails

### Design principles (revised, brand-anchored)

1. **Architecture is the content; measured reality is the subject.** *Why:* honesty and craft, shown not claimed, never date.
2. **Measured reality is warm.** Precision is always balanced by warmth, personality, and human craftsmanship; the technology documents architecture, it never replaces it. *Why:* it keeps us "an architecture studio using amazing technology," never a "cool technology company"; the instrument reveals the emotion of the work, it is not the hero.
3. **Two values in balance: calm and discovery.** *Why:* calm earns trust; discovery embodies the proposition; each needs the other.
4. **Less interface, more content.** *Why:* the work persuades and is remembered.
5. **Editorial discipline, not editorial aesthetic.** *Why:* the discipline is timeless; the print look would make us the reference.
6. **Asymmetry with balance.** *Why:* movement and life, held in order.
7. **Scale and focus create hierarchy; focus is the universal mechanism.** *Why:* one coherent, digital-native grammar of curation.
8. **Silence is space and time.** *Why:* calm is composed emptiness *and* unhurried, precise change.
9. **Storytelling is image-led and inspection-led; reward curiosity.** *Why:* evidence and exploration outperform explanation.
10. **Consistency in system, variety in composition.** *Why:* a hidden consistent system makes variety feel authored.
11. **Motion is a primary material, used deliberately — never mandatory, never entertainment.** *Why:* truthful to a measurement craft and a tool of discovery, kept from becoming decoration.
12. **Blue is semantic activation, never decoration.** *Why:* a meaningful signal reads as precision; a rationed signal stays powerful.
13. **Pixel type is a voice and a coordinate marker, never the body.** *Why:* disciplined identity, tied to the craft.
14. **The mascot is an adaptive character, never a logo drop.** *Why:* personality with a structural job — and the warmth that keeps the technology human.
15. **White is the working ground; a darker capture environment is a candidate, not yet a rule.** *Why:* protect the ground; validate the dark environment before elevating it.
16. **Timeless over trendy; instrument-calm, not print-still.** *Why:* durability is the definition of timeless, and our calm is exact, not merely quiet.

### Composition grammar

A loose underlying grid (12/16-column kind) governs everything but is never visible. Align to it; **break it intentionally and selectively.** Prefer **edge alignment** over centring. Create tension through **offset, scale contrast, and asymmetric balance.** Allow images to **bleed to the canvas edge.** Hierarchy may be **static or interactive (focus).** Alternate deliberately between composition types — dominant image + small support; a marker/data block with strong asymmetric margins; an image sequence of varying scale; small elements in large space; a mixed composition with slight overlap — so no two consecutive moments match. *Why:* a rigorous invisible order makes freedom legible; alternation produces rhythm and defeats the template feeling.

### Things we never do

Never let the interface or the technology become the hero in place of the architecture. Never let precision read as cold or clinical — warmth, personality, and craft are always present. Never present items at equal weight when one could be in focus. Never use blue as area, background, fill, or decoration, or on stacks of buttons. Never let the pixel voice become body text or scatter as decoration. Never reduce the mascot to a logo dropped in corners. Never build symmetrical, centred, landing-page sections. Never repeat the same layout in succession. Never present work as a uniform grid or product slider. Never let the interface dominate the work. Never over-explain a moment with long paragraphs. Never use filled, button-heavy CTAs or the SaaS/agency vocabulary. Never use heavy filled bands as default containers. Never use decorative gradients or backgrounds. Never make the visitor a passive observer where inspection is possible. Never use motion to entertain; never loop, pulse, or perform; never make a single motion load-bearing. Never crop an image merely to fit a slot. Never treat the technical work as a gadget demo. Never use stock-like filler imagery. Never reproduce the reference's actual layouts, type, colour, or interactions. Never let responsive views collapse into one monotonous stack that erases art-direction.

### Candidates to validate through prototyping

*Promising directions not yet elevated to principles; to be tested in practice before adoption.*

- **The dual white/dark environment.** A deliberate dark, technical environment for the reality-capture work (where point clouds and scans glow) set against the white design ground. Potentially our strongest expression of the two pillars and our best density/silence contrast — but it must be seen in prototype before becoming a rule. Until validated, white remains the working ground and dark fields are used sparingly and deliberately.
- **The scan-reveal gesture at scale** and **the explorable point cloud as the signature centerpiece** are approved directions; their exact behaviour should still be proven in prototype for calmness and performance.

### Acceptance checklist

A future implementation passes only if every statement is true:

- Every frozen brand element is present and recognizable; someone who knows the current design sees the **same brand**, matured.
- The metaphor is legible: the site feels like **a precise instrument for exploring measured reality**, not a printed publication.
- **The technology never becomes the hero:** precision is balanced by warmth, personality, and human craftsmanship, and the architecture (light, texture, space, people, stories) is always what the instrument reveals. It reads as an architecture studio using amazing technology, never as a technology company.
- Both values are present: it is **calm** *and* it **invites discovery** (inspection, zoom, focus, exploration) — the visitor is not a passive observer.
- **Blue appears only as activation/measurement** (identity, the one action, focus, reveal) — never as area or decoration, never on button stacks.
- **Focus is the hierarchy mechanism:** wherever multiple items exist, one leads and the rest recede; there is no uniform equal grid by default.
- **Motion is deliberate and instrument-grade** — slow, weighted, clarifying, reduced-motion honored, none load-bearing — and used where it aids understanding or exploration.
- Pixel type is identity/coordinate only, with breathing room; a calm reading voice carries text; metadata reads as precise measurement data.
- The mascot is used as a character with intention and space.
- Rhythm alternates **density and silence in space and time**; pacing is not metronomic; compositions are asymmetric and edge-weighted; images bleed selectively; nothing is centred-by-default.
- There is **no filled CTA block, no blue social-button strip, no utility footer, and no heavy filled band as a default container.**
- Text is reduced; imagery and inspection carry meaning; no moment over-explains.
- Projects read as an **explorable, focus-driven collection** (carousels, irregular composition, inspectable project pages with zoom), never a portfolio grid.
- The dark environment, if used, is treated as a **validated prototype outcome**, not assumed.
- The result feels like a contemporary architecture + digital-measurement studio — **unmistakably *diverse anumite*** — and not like a Webflow/Framer/SaaS/marketing template.
- Every element can be justified as serving the work; every notable decision would still be correct in ten years.

## Closing statement

The identity was never the problem; the metaphor was. Re-rooted in **measured reality** — warm, never cold — governed by **calm and discovery**, and equipped with a real operating system — blue as activation, focus as hierarchy, motion as a deliberate material, inspection as the native interaction — the visual language is now unmistakably ours. It keeps every element the designer built and teaches them to behave with the confidence of an instrument: to say less, to leave space and time, to let one thing come into focus, to invite the visitor to look closer, and to spend its electric blue like a signal, not a coat of paint. The technology is there to reveal the architecture — the light, the texture, the space, the people — never to replace it. Built faithfully to this, the site cannot be a template — it can only be *diverse anumite*, matured.
